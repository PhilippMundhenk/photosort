What moves, and when

photosort only ever proposes. These switches decide whether a proposal needs your approval in Review or is carried out by itself.

The safety catch. While it is on, nothing is moved, copied or deleted, whatever you approve: approvals are only noted. Switch it off when the proposals look right; everything you approved until then is moved at that moment, and from then on an approval moves its photos straight away.

At the end of every run, a finished trip goes into {{ cfg.root }}/<name>/ without waiting in Review. Photos whose position is doubtful are parked in <name>/{{ cfg.review_dir }}/ for you to check. A trip still under way (no photo at home yet) always waits. Only applies while dry-run is off.

The same for day outs: an outing of a few hours near or far from home becomes its folder at the end of the run. Leave it off if you would rather look at each one first, since a day out is the kind of cluster most often proposed by mistake.

A burst of photos at home (a birthday, a barbecue) goes into {{ cfg.unnamed_dir }}/<date> (n Fotos)/ and waits there for a name; you give it one under “Bursts waiting for a name” in Review. Off: the burst waits in Review before anything moves.

Photos that belong to no cluster are filed by month into {{ cfg.everyday_layout }}/ at the end of every run, once they are older than the number of days below. Off: they stay in the inbox until you press the button on the Everyday page.{% if cfg.everyday_layout == 'leave' %} The everyday layout is leave, so nothing is moved either way.{% endif %}

A photo from yesterday may still become part of a trip or an occasion once more photos arrive, so everyday photos are only filed away after this many days. Four is enough for phones that sync daily; raise it if a device syncs once a week.

Clusters receive copies and the originals stay in the inbox, marked as sorted so they are not proposed again. Useful while you do not trust the sorting yet, or when another tool still needs the inbox; it costs the disk space twice. Undo deletes the copies.

How often the inboxes are checked for new photos and the proposals are brought up to date. A scan with nothing new takes moments; “Run now” in the header starts one at once.

Shows a note on the pages when an inbox and the sorted folder are on different mounts, because every move is then a copy over the network instead of a rename. Switch it off when they are different shares of one NAS and cannot share a mount.

Next to every sorted photo an .xmp file receives keywords such as the zone and the cluster name, which photo managers (digiKam, Lightroom, Synology Photos) can read. Switch it off if you do not want extra files beside your photos.

Folders

Where new photos arrive and where sorted ones go. Paths are those inside the container.

The folder your devices sync into. Every subfolder is one input, named after it, so a new phone needs no configuration: create its folder and the next scan picks it up. Where clusters are created, one folder each: {{ cfg.root }}/2026-08 Singapore/. The container must be able to write here. If it is on the same mount as the inbox, a move is an instant rename.

The folder pattern inside the sorted folder for photos that are in no cluster. YYYY/MM files them by year and month (2026/08/); YYYY by year only; leave never moves them out of the inbox.

Only needed when your inputs are not subfolders of one inbox folder. One per line, name=/path. Leave it empty to use the subfolders found above.

Inside each cluster, photos are kept apart by the input they came from: 2026-10 Lisbon/phone-a/ and 2026-10 Lisbon/phone-b/ instead of one flat folder. Handy when two people's photos should stay separate.

Inside the sorted folder. A burst at home that was moved before you named it waits here; naming it in Review moves it to its final place.

Inside a cluster that was moved without asking, photos with a low confidence (a borrowed or missing position) are parked in this subfolder. A cluster you approved by hand has none: your approval settles them.

The extensions treated as photos and videos, separated by commas. Anything else in the inbox is left alone.

Home

Everything is decided by where a photo was taken relative to home: at home, near home, or away.

An IANA name such as Europe/Berlin. Photo times that carry no offset are read in it, and video times (stored in UTC) are converted to it, so an evening stays one evening.

Latitude and longitude of your home. If you leave both at 0, the next run finds it: the spot where photos were taken on the most different days. “Detect” does the same right now. A home that is a few kilometres off turns every day into a day out, so check it once.

Photos closer to home than this count as taken at home: they are everyday photos, and any of them ends a trip. Half a kilometre covers the house, the garden and GPS drift; raise it on a farm, lower it in a dense city if the park next door should count as an outing.

Beyond home but closer than this is local, further is away. Near home an outing needs more photos to become a day out (the school run must stay everyday), and a night without photos ends it, because you sleep at home.

A second flat, the grandparents' house, a cabin: places where photos are everyday photos too, and where a trip ends. One per line, Name = latitude, longitude, radius km.

Place names

Folder names come from an offline map of towns. Your own places take precedence.

Photos inside one of these circles are labelled with its name instead of the nearest town; the nearest circle wins. One per line, Name = latitude, longitude, radius km. Ticking “remember this place” when you rename a cluster adds one from its photos.

Places with fewer inhabitants are named after their region instead, so a trip is not called after a hamlet nobody knows. Lower it if you want village names.

Trips and day outs

An excursion is a run of photos away from home, ended by any photo at home. These numbers decide what it becomes.

An excursion spanning at least this long is a trip (you stayed overnight); a shorter one is a day out. Twenty hours separates a long day from a night away.

The fewest photos with a position for a trip to be proposed. A day out far from home needs as many. Fewer, and the photos stay everyday.

Near home, two photos at the supermarket are not an outing. Only a run with at least this many photos is proposed as a day out; raise it if errands keep showing up in Review.

Near home you sleep at home, even if no photo proves it. A gap longer than this between two photos in the local zone ends the outing, so Saturday's walk and Sunday's are two day outs.

Two journeys with no photo at home in between would be one trip. A gap longer than this splits them, but only if the two sides are also far apart (the distance below): a quiet week at the same beach stays one trip.

Used with the gap above, and to decide whether two phones were on the same trip: photos further apart than this are in different areas.

A trip is named after the places passed, in order: 2026-06 Lisbon, Sevilla. With more places than this the countries are used instead, and beyond that “Multiple”.

On: 2026-08 Singapore. Off: 2026-08-08..29 Singapore, with the exact days. A single day is always written out in full.

Occasions at home

Many photos at home in a short time are probably an occasion. Each one is proposed for you to name or reject; metadata cannot tell a birthday from twenty shots of the cat.

Photos at home closer together than this belong to the same burst. Three hours keeps an afternoon party together and separates it from the evening.

Smaller bursts are never proposed. Raise it if too many ordinary evenings show up in Review; lower it if small occasions are missed.

A burst must also have this many times the photos you usually take at home in a day. Someone who photographs a lot at home needs more for an occasion than someone who rarely does; the usual day is measured from your own library.

Thumbnails

The pages show small previews. The NAS usually has them already.

A path relative to each photo; {name} stands for the file name. The default is Synology's layout. When a thumbnail exists there it is used as it is, which costs nothing.

Missing previews are generated in the background and kept in data/thumbs (videos need ffmpeg). Off: photos without a NAS thumbnail show a placeholder, and the box has less to do.

Records

For every photo in the inbox a small record is kept: time, position, place. It can always be rebuilt, since a photo without one is simply read again on the next scan.

Central keeps them in the data volume (data/index/<inbox>/…) and leaves your photo folders clean. Beside writes a file next to each photo, which follows the photo wherever another tool moves it.

{name} is the photo's file name, {stem} the name without its extension, {ext} the extension. A pattern that would overwrite the photo itself is ignored.

Its record is no longer needed, because the cluster folder's manifest.json holds everything about it. Keep the record only if another tool of yours reads it.

Maintenance

After changing where records are kept, move the existing ones there. Removing stale records deletes those of photos already sorted and those whose photo is gone.
